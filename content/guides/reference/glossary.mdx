# Glossary

The words this app uses, and exactly what each one means here. Where a word has a loose
everyday meaning, the strict one is what the screens use.

## ballot

A club's draw for tickets. You enter it; you do not buy from it. Entering early is not
entering better — a ballot is a draw, and Fetch.io's job is to get every account entered
before the window closes, not to get you a better place in it.

## pool

The accounts available to ballot runs: everything belonging to one of the seven supported
clubs. `01_pool` is the tab, and "the pool" is that set of accounts, not the table showing
it.

The pool is the whole set — not the page of twenty-five you are looking at. "20 of 48
ready" counts the pool.

## account

One club login: an email, a password, a club, and whatever else you know about it — a
membership, a proxy, a mailbox, tags. The email is its identity; duplicates are matched
on it.

## status

What happened the last time Fetch.io tried to sign in with an account: `ACTIVE`,
`NEEDS_LOGIN`, `NEEDS_OTP`, `LOCKED`, `EXPIRED` or `ERROR`. A statement about the past,
with a `LAST_CHECK` timestamp saying how far past.

## ready

Whether an account can enter a ballot **right now, under a specific profile**. Broader
than status: session valid, not locked, membership live, plus a proxy and a mailbox if
that profile needs them.

The same account can be READY for one profile and BLOCKED for another. Both places that
show it name the profile they measured against.

## profile

A set of pacing rules: how many accounts at once, how long between attempts, how many
retries, which proxy group, where one-time codes come from, and whether to stop on the
first rate limit. It holds no accounts and no clubs.

A run records its profile's name and settings when it starts, so editing a profile never
changes what a past run did.

## run

One pairing of accounts and a profile, at a moment. It has a status
(`QUEUED`, `RUNNING`, `PAUSED`, `COMPLETED`, `STOPPED`, `FAILED`), counts, a rate, an ETA
and a log. It lives on the backend: closing the tab does not stop it.

Runs accumulate. They are the history of what you attempted and what the clubs said.

## task

One account's attempt inside a run — one row of the monitor's table. A task has a status,
an attempt count, and the last response the club gave. A run of 48 accounts has 48 tasks.

## entry

The thing being submitted: one account's ballot entry for one club. A confirmed entry
gets a reference like `EN-5_0010`, which is the club's receipt and the only proof the
entry landed.

"Attempt" is what Fetch.io does; "entry" is what the club records.

## attempt

One try at a task. `2/3` in the ATTEMPT column means the second of up to three, and a
retried attempt starts from scratch — new sign-in, new submission.

## seq

The number on every event in a run's log. It only ever increases, and the app asks for
"everything after seq N" rather than re-reading the whole log, which is what lets the
monitor keep a growing feed without duplicating lines.

Two guarantees, which matter when reading a log during an incident: `seq` ascending is
also time ascending, and a cursor never goes backwards.

## worker

One of the concurrent slots a run has. Concurrency 8 means eight workers, each taking the
next account, entering it, waiting the profile's delay, and taking another.

## rate

Attempts completed per minute, measured rather than predicted: what has actually happened
divided by elapsed time. It falls when a club slows down, which is usually the first
visible sign that one has.
